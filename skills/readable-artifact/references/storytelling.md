# Storytelling

There is no template. The order of scenes comes from the reader's questions about this content, and each scene's visual comes from the shape of its answer. Two pages of the same type can and should have different scenes.

## Turn the reader's questions into scenes

1. **List every question the reader will have** while reading this page. Write them in the reader's words, not yours. Include the question they open the page with.
2. **Order them by understanding.** Put a question after the questions whose answers it depends on. If answer B makes no sense until answer A is known, A comes first.
3. **Merge questions that share one answer.** Two scenes that would say the same thing are one scene.
4. **Drop questions the reader does not need answered to do what they will do after reading.**
5. **Each remaining question becomes one scene**, and its answer becomes the scene's heading.

Use the fewest scenes that deliver the point. Never add a scene to look thorough.

## Ordering tendencies

These usually hold. Break them when the reader's questions say otherwise.

- The general case comes before its exception.
- What changes for the reader comes after they understand the main point.

## Choose the visual by the shape of the answer

Look at the answer, not the document type, and pick the visual that has the same shape. The table lists common visuals, not the only ones; use any visual that shows the point more clearly.

| Shape of the answer | Visual |
| --- | --- |
| Things happen in order | Timeline (rules can attach to the step where they apply) |
| A judgment branches on a condition | Flow with a fork |
| Several items compared on the same criteria | Table |
| Who is responsible for what | Role cards, each with the question that role answers |
| Before and after, allowed and not allowed | Two cards side by side |
| Several conditions that must all hold | Condition chips or an unnumbered checklist |

Notes:

- If an answer has two shapes, it is two questions. Split the scene.
- A table for a sequence hides the order; a timeline for a comparison hides the criteria. Match the shape.
- If no shape fits, the answer is one claim with no parts. The heading alone states it, or the scene merges into a neighbor.

## Check the scenes

Step 3 is done when all of these hold.

- The first scene states the main point and what the page asks of the reader, before any background.
- Each scene makes sense without reading a later scene.
- No two scenes make the same point.
- Each scene is needed for what the reader will do after reading. Without it, the reader could not decide, act, or know what changed for them.
